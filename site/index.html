Vinyl ADC — Audiophile Discrete Converter

Zero dedicated ADC ICs in the analog signal path. Pure discrete continuous-time conversion.

An audiophile-grade stereo analog-to-digital converter engineered entirely from standard analog op-amps (TL072), precision comparators (LM311), and 74HC CMOS logic—completely eliminating proprietary single-chip ADC ICs from the analog signal chain. Designed specifically for high-fidelity turntable digitization between a cartridge phono stage and a Raspberry Pi, it converts incoming analog signals directly into a continuous-time 1.536 MHz bitstream with third-order noise shaping (+60 dB/decade) and delivers bit-perfect 24-bit / 48 kHz Linear PCM FLAC via software CIC and FIR decimation.

How It Was Built: Engineering Pipeline

From SPICE simulation and KiCad PCB layout through parametric mechanical CAD to photorealistic Blender studio rendering.

Step 1

KiCad 10 Design & SRM-CAM

Modular 4-board architecture (100 × 100 mm each) engineered for single-sided isolation milling on a Roland SRM-20 CNC mill via SRM-CAM. Star-ground architecture isolates analog audio returns from digital switching noise.

Step 2

3D CAD Board Export

Exported using kicad-cli pcb export step and glb with native footprints and 3D packages (DIP-8, DIP-14, DIP-16, DIP-20, axial resistors, capacitors, and stacking headers).

Step 3

Parametric Enclosure

Parametrically modeled in OpenSCAD with 0.5 mm board clearance on all sides, 3.0 mm wall thickness, M3 mounting bosses at (±44 mm, ±44 mm), and cutouts matching connector coordinates.

Step 4

Blender Studio Rendering

Assembled in Blender with 3-point soft area lighting, physical material nodes (matte PLA roughness 0.48, crystal clear acrylic transmission 0.95, polished brass), and rendered at 1920 × 1080.
