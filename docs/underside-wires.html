Take both rails off their single header joint

The right channel's loop is not wrong. Its negative rail arrives through solder that has wicked up one unplated hole at bus pin J7.16 — under the header body, where no iron reaches — and that joint lets go when the stack is pressed together. Two insulated wires on the solder side make both rails independent of it — on both channel boards, four wires in all.

01The two wires

J7.16 → U22.4 -5V · 50 mm apart

The entire negative rail enters here. Its only copper is a component-side trace to U22 pin 4, reached through an unplated hole under the header body, and the stack flexes that joint every time it is pushed together.

J7.2 → U23.14 +5V · 21 mm apart

The same arrangement on the positive rail: one component-side joint feeds U21, C27 and R37. It has not failed yet, and it is the same joint.

The whole solder side is a bare ground pour

These boards are milled, so there is no solder mask: an uninsulated wire lying across the underside shorts the rail it carries straight to ground. Use insulated wire — 30 AWG Kynar or any thin stranded hook-up wire — and strip only the last 2 mm at each end.

02Fitting them

With the bus header off the board, these pads are completely clear — this is the easiest moment to do it, so fit the wires before the header goes back.

  1. Work on the board alone, solder side up. If the header is still fitted, unplug the board from the stack and leave the header in place; the pads are reachable from below either way.
  2. Cut the −5 V wire about 60 mm long, the +5 V wire about 30 mm. Straight-line spans are 50 mm and 21 mm; the extra is slack for routing.
  3. Tin the four pads and both wire ends first. The header pins take heat well from below; the socket pins need a moment longer.
  4. Solder the −5 V wire: J7 pin 16 to U22 pin 4. In the view at left, J7 pin 16 is the corner pin at the right-hand end of the header's inner row.
  5. Solder the +5 V wire: J7 pin 2 to U23 pin 14. U23 pin 14 is the corner of the 74HC74 nearest the header.
  6. Press the wires flat and tack them down. They must not stand proud enough to touch the neighbouring board in the stack.
  7. If the header came off, refit it and solder all 16 pins from the solder side only. Check the sixteen copper rings on the solder side first — a ring that lifted during desoldering breaks whatever it fed, and pins 4 to 14 each carry a reference or a clock.
  8. Repeat all of it on the other channel board.

Nothing on this header needs soldering on the component side once the wires are in. Fourteen of the sixteen pins never did: the eight ground pins meet the pour, and pins 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 and 14 each reach their destination through bottom copper. Only pins 2 and 16 were carried by a top-side joint, and that is what the two wires replace.

Why a wire, when the trace is already there

The trace was never the problem

Copper is copper: the run from J7.16 to U22.4 carries the rail perfectly well. The question is how the current gets from the header pin into that copper at all — and on this board there is only one answer, and it is a bad one.

These holes are drilled, not plated. Nothing joins the two faces of the board. The solder-side ring at J7.16 is an island connected to nothing, and the trace leaves from the component-side ring, which the header body covers: the body outline clears the pad by 0.42 mm, so once the header is seated there is no reaching that joint with an iron. What has been carrying the negative rail all along is solder that wicked 1.6 mm up the 0.1 mm gap between the pin and the hole wall — invisible, unverifiable, and squeezed every time the stack is pressed together. That is why reflowing pin 16 from below never held: it was reflowing a joint it could not see.

Bridging the pin to the trace on the component side would be electrically sound, and it would still need a joint on that pin, on that face, under that body, taking that same flex — and whatever you leave standing up there is what the next board in the stack presses against.

18 September · hunting the DACP_L short

Where a short to ground can hide on this net

With U24 out of its socket, socket pin 4 still reads short to ground, so the fault is in the board's own copper. DACP_L is only three pads and two runs, but the solder-side half is about 45 mm of bare trace with the ground pour 0.5 mm away along both edges and no mask in between. Solid red is solder-side copper, dashed is component-side.

Search in this order: U24 pins 4 and 5 first — they are adjacent and pin 5 is ground, so one bridge does it, and the socket's own contacts count too. Then R25.1, where the component-side run meets the solder-side trace. Then the trace itself, following it up to R34.1. A whisker across 0.5 mm of bare copper is the likeliest thing you will find.

04Then ask the bitstream

Boot the Pi first, then power the ADC. Stop the ripper so it releases the capture card, and run the live view on the same port:

ssh mads@vinyladc.local
sudo systemctl stop vinyl-adc-ripper
python3 ~/pi/live.py          # then open http://vinyladc.local:8091

The right channel should stop looking like the middle column and start looking like the left one. Mean run length is the quickest tell — a limit cycle shows as long solid bars in the bit view.
