## What SPICE is for here, and what it is not

The Python model already predicts **68 dB SNR at −8 dBFS** and does it properly,
with long runs and an FFT. **Do not try to reproduce that number in SPICE.**
Reaching 68 dB over a 20 kHz band at 1.536 MHz and OSR 32 needs on the order of
a second of transient at nanosecond steps; it would run for hours and tell you
what `sim/verify.py` already knows.

SPICE's job is the analog reality the Python model idealises:

- op-amp GBW and slew against the TL074's real limits
- the LM311's 200 ns delay and its single-supply threshold
- the charge pump's droop under real load
- where the op-amps actually saturate — which is load-bearing (see below)

Say so on the sheets, so nobody later reads a missing SNR figure as a gap.

## Benches to build

One per block plus a whole-loop sheet, each its own KiCad project. Suggested,
but derive the final list from the schematic's own blocks:

| bench | asserts |
|---|---|
| rails + charge pump | −5 V pump from the 74HC244 with all eight outputs paralleled reaches **≈ −4.1 V at ~32 mA**, ripple **< 1 µV** after 4.7 Ω + 220 µF; pump clocked at **192 kHz** from 4040 Q5 |
| reference | `VREF_P` sits at **2.5 V**; the −2.5 V reference tracks the rail (that is deliberate — it turns rail noise into −98 dB gain modulation instead of additive noise) |
| clock divider | 1.536 MHz in, **4040 Q5 = 192 kHz**, correct division and duty |
| one integrator | gain matches its design coefficient with a real TL074, `a = [0.250, 0.320, 0.610]` normalised to fs |
| quantiser | LM311 on **single +5 V/GND**, summing node biased to +2.5 V, ~200 ns propagation |
| DAC + ELD | 74HC04 swinging 0/5 V into `R_d` with an equal resistor to −2.5 V gives net **±2.5 V**; ELD compensation `k0 = −0.225` |
| **whole loop, one channel** | the payoff sheet — see below |

## The whole-loop bench is the one that matters

Three things to check, in order of importance:

1. **The click test.** A 40 µs transient at 3× full scale, then a normal signal.
   The loop **must recover**. A 1-bit third-order loop that overloads never comes
   back on its own, and on vinyl that is not a corner case. This is the single
   most valuable thing SPICE can tell you here that the schematic cannot.
2. **Where the TL074 actually saturates.** The design has **no clamp parts** — the
   integrator state scales are chosen so the op-amps' own output saturation *is*
   the clamp, at roughly ±2.7 V on ±5 V rails, i.e. 1.3–1.8× Vref. If real
   saturation lands outside that window the rescue mechanism does not work.
   **The rail voltages and the resistor scaling are load-bearing, not arbitrary.**
3. **Integrator swings at −8 dBFS**: 1.43 / 1.47 / 1.45 V. Full scale is
   3.49 Vpk (2.47 Vrms) with the level trimmer at maximum, set by `Rin = 20k5`
   against `Rd1 = 14k7`.

Assert against **those numbers from `design-notes.md`, not against whatever your
models happen to do.** A model and a test that share the same wrong assumption
agree with each other and are both wrong — that has already happened once on the
Class D board, where an inverted comparator model passed its own test.
